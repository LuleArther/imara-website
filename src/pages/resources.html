Health Tips for Every Family

General information in simple language. It does not replace advice from a health worker.

Danger signs: go to a health facility now

Do not wait, and do not wait for a reply from Imara, if you or someone near you has:

  • Difficulty breathing, or chest pain
  • Fits (convulsions), fainting, or being very sleepy and hard to wake
  • Heavy bleeding, including bleeding in pregnancy
  • A very high fever in a baby or young child, or a child who cannot drink or breastfeed
  • Severe pain, a serious injury, or sudden weakness on one side of the body

Go to the nearest health facility immediately or ask someone to take you.

Malaria: test early, treat early
  • Fever, headache, chills, body pain and vomiting can be signs of malaria.
  • Get tested at a health facility before taking malaria medicine. Fever is not always malaria.
  • If the test is positive, finish all the medicine, even when you feel better.
  • Children under five and pregnant women can become very sick quickly — get them tested the same day.
  • Sleep under a treated mosquito net every night and clear standing water around your home.
Pregnancy: start antenatal care early
  • Visit a health facility for antenatal care as soon as you think you are pregnant, and keep going to every visit.
  • Plan to give birth at a health facility with a trained health worker.
  • Go straight away if you have bleeding, severe headache, swelling of the face or hands, fever, or the baby stops moving.
Child health: protect your child
  • Take your child for every immunisation on their child health card.
  • Breastfeed and give clean, safe drinking water.
  • Diarrhoea: keep giving fluids and take the child to a health worker, especially if they are weak or not drinking.
  • A child with fast breathing or a cough that will not go away should be checked by a health worker.
HIV testing: know your status
  • Testing is confidential. Knowing your status helps you protect yourself and the people you love.
  • People living with HIV can live long, healthy lives with treatment, which is available at government health facilities in Uganda.
  • Choosing “HIV Testing” on our Get Care form only tells us which service you want. We never assume anyone's status.
Mental health: it is okay to ask for help
  • Feeling sad, worried or hopeless for a long time, not sleeping, or finding it hard to cope are reasons to talk to someone.
  • Talking to someone you trust — a friend, family member, religious leader or health worker — can help.
  • If you are thinking of harming yourself, go to a health facility now or tell someone near you immediately.

Clean hands, clean water
  • Wash hands with soap and water after using the toilet and before preparing food or eating.
  • Boil or treat drinking water, and store it in a clean, covered container.
  • Use a latrine and keep it clean.

This information is general and is not a diagnosis. If you are unwell, talk to a health worker or ask Imara to link you to care.
